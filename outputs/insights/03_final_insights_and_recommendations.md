# Final Insights and Business Recommendations

These three final insights were selected after reviewing the consumer behavior, segmentation and promotion-analysis evidence together.

The recommendations are evidence-based decision proposals. They do not claim measured profitability, incremental sales or causal promotion lift from the historical dataset.

## Final Insight 1 — Revenue concentration creates a clear retention priority

**Insight**  
Revenue concentration creates a clear retention priority.

**Evidence**  
High-Value Frequent Shoppers represent 40.28% of customers but contribute 73.52% of total sales. Their average customer sales are $5,882.46, purchase frequency is 9.17 baskets per active month, and average recency is 5.32 days.

**Business interpretation**  
A deterioration in engagement within this segment would expose a disproportionate share of the current revenue base. Retention monitoring should therefore focus on changes in recent purchase behavior rather than treating all customers equally.

**Target segment**  
High-Value Frequent Shoppers

**Recommended action**  
Create a retention-monitoring rule for this segment using recency and purchase-frequency deterioration, and trigger category-relevant re-engagement when customers move materially away from their established shopping pattern. Do not default to broad discounting.

**KPI**  
90-day repeat purchase rate

## Final Insight 2 — Large-basket occasional shoppers have frequency-growth headroom

**Insight**  
Large-basket occasional shoppers have frequency-growth headroom.

**Evidence**  
Large-Basket Occasional Shoppers represent 36.08% of customers and 21.49% of total sales. They have the highest average basket value at $40.07, but purchase frequency is only 2.94 baskets per active month. Their median average items per basket is 15.59, versus 12.13 for High-Value Frequent Shoppers.

**Business interpretation**  
This segment already exhibits substantial shopping missions when it visits. The more relevant behavioral gap is visit frequency, not basket expansion.

**Target segment**  
Large-Basket Occasional Shoppers

**Recommended action**  
Use replenishment, next-visit and category-relevant engagement to encourage additional shopping occasions without making larger baskets the primary objective.

**KPI**  
Purchase frequency (baskets per active month)

---

## Final Insight 3 — Promotion-basket exposure overstates line-level promotion dependence

**Insight**  
Promotion-basket exposure overstates line-level promotion dependence.

**Evidence**  
Large-Basket Occasional Shoppers have the highest mean promotion purchase share at 89.94%, yet their mean promotion spend share is 51.36%. High-Value Frequent Shoppers show 85.37% promotion purchase share and 51.21% promotion spend share. The exploratory promotion-spend-share effect size is only epsilon-squared=0.0275.

**Business interpretation**  
The presence of a promotional item in a basket is not a reliable proxy for the share of customer sales actually generated by promotional lines. Promotion targeting should therefore use line-level dependence and category context, not basket exposure alone.

**Target segment**  
Large-Basket Occasional Shoppers

**Recommended action**  
When using promotions for this segment, prioritize category-specific targeted offers and evaluate them against a holdout rather than escalating broad discounting solely because promotional-basket exposure is high.

**KPI**  
Incremental repeat purchase rate versus holdout

---

## Selection Notes

The small historical high-value/high-recency screening pocket remains analytically valid but was not selected as a final portfolio-level insight because it contains only 18 customers (0.72% of the customer base).

Category-level promotion patterns remain supporting evidence rather than a standalone final insight. `COUPON/MISC ITEMS` is retained for reconciliation but is not interpreted as a strategic merchandise category.

The segment × top-category chi-square test was not used because expected-count assumptions were inadequate.
